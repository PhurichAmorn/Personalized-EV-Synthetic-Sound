# Shrink the space, then let listeners steer

The strongest evidence points away from rating-driven evolution over raw synthesis parameters. It points toward **preferential Bayesian optimization (PBO) or slider-based line search over a compact continuous space of about 6–12 dimensions**, filtered beforehand by computable comfort checks and warm-started from earlier participants. Granular synthesis is a sound engine to keep. It is the documented production method at Hyundai and a standard option in supplier toolchains. Its problems in this prototype trace to two choices: one grain configuration shared by every sound, and blending between a few presets. The literature's fix is state-driven grain selection from a descriptor-analyzed corpus, with grain settings tuned per source. Offline text-to-audio generation can enlarge that corpus. On who prefers what, the repeated finding is that **listeners split into subgroups** (ICE-like, novel synthetic, and quiet or authentic) rather than sharing one optimum. That supports per-user search, though age, culture and personality remain untested. Uncomfortable combinations can be prevented in large part before anyone hears them. Lock layer pitch ratios, cap loudness, sharpness, roughness and tone prominence on a rendered speed sweep, and learn a "reject" classifier from listeners. Comfort metrics predict synthetic-sound pleasantness only weakly, so they should act as guards, not as the objective. Most of the evidence comes from images, music, or ICE engine sound. No published study applies these methods to time-varying, vehicle-state-dependent EV sound, so EV Sound Lab would be testing the transfer itself.

## IEC's ceiling is fatigue and dimensionality, not the rating scale

The symptoms the team reports (low diversity, unsatisfied users, slow progress) match IEC's known limits. Takagi's survey and McDermott's timbre work put practical IEC at roughly **10–20 generations before fatigue** ([McDermott et al., Galway](https://research.universityofgalway.ie/en/publications/interactive-ec-control-of-synthesized-timbre-3/)). Fukumoto and Hanada spread IEC sound evaluation over several days to cope ([SMC 2024](https://smcnetwork.org/smc2024/papers/SMC2024_paper_id158.pdf)). Two independent 1–7 scores per pair cost the listener a lot and say little about direction in a space with dozens of parameters. Switching to pairwise choice alone does not fix that, because a pairwise choice is one bit about the whole vector.

The preset-blend design deepens the diversity problem. Blending between hand-made presets is in effect a hand-built low-dimensional embedding, so every candidate lies inside the convex hull of a few starting sounds. The search cannot discover anything outside that hull. The project already runs a GP-preference + BO method beside the evolutionary one, so the question is not whether to adopt BO. The question is **what space it searches and what feedback feeds it**.

## Line search and galleries make 10+ dimensions tractable in a session

The best-supported alternative is human-in-the-loop BO over a compact space, using feedback richer than a rating. Koyama's **Sequential Line Search** gives the user one slider per iteration along a line that BO chooses in n-D space, and feeds the slider position into a Bradley–Terry preference GP ([Koyama et al. 2017](https://koyama.xyz/project/sequential_line_search/)). **Sequential Gallery** asks the user to pick the best cell in a 2-D plane ([Koyama et al. 2020](https://koyama.xyz/project/sequential_gallery)). It still needs about **10–15 iterations to converge**, and Meta-PO cuts that to **about 6–8** by reusing earlier users' preference models ([Li, Liao & Holz, UIST 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15355)). BO-as-Assistant keeps free manual sliders and shows BO suggestions alongside them, which preserves the user's sense of control ([Koyama & Goto, UIST 2022](https://www.koyama.xyz/project/bo-as-assistant/)).

Audio has one practical difference from images: listening is serial. A 5×5 image gallery takes a glance, while a 25-clip audio gallery takes minutes. A slider, or a small gallery of 4–6 sounds, fits audio better. EV Sound Lab renders in real time, so a slider moved during a looping scripted drive is feasible. Two caveats apply. That pairing is project interpretation, not published EV practice. And GP-based BO degrades beyond roughly 10–20 dimensions, so the search space must be compressed first.

The closest audio evidence comes from music and synthesizers. **ROMBO** searched a 256-D music-GAN latent through a 10-D rotated embedding. With 16 participants and 64 clips in about 30 minutes, the BO group found new favourites about **40% more often and 16% faster** than random sampling ([Marcos et al., PLoS One 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12637961/)). A DRS 2022 paper reports that BO in a 2-D VAE latent of synth parameters reduced the number of listening trials needed. Its effect sizes could not be verified ([DRS 2022](https://dl.designresearchsociety.org/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=3072&context=drs-conference-papers)). No head-to-head study of IEC against PBO on synth timbre was found. **Whether users end up more satisfied, and not just faster, is unmeasured**, so this project should treat satisfaction as an explicit outcome.

The compact space can be built three ways. The cheapest is a **random or rotated embedding** (REMBO/ROMBO) of the existing parameters, which needs no training. The second is a learned latent: a VAE or flow trained on many random but valid engine parameter sets, ideally regularized so that latent distance tracks perceived timbre. Esling's generative timbre spaces do this with perceptual dissimilarity ratings ([Esling et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.08501v2)). FlowSynth maps such a latent back to synthesizer parameters and exposes disentangled axes as macro-controls ([Esling et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00971v1)). The third is a set of **learned semantic knobs**. AdaptiveKnobs fits a GP from parameters to a user's sense of "bright" or "scratchy" and builds one knob for each quality ([Huang et al., IUI 2014](https://kgajos.seas.harvard.edu/papers/huang14knobs.pdf)). Whichever route the team takes, the training data should be **sampled from the full parameter space rather than from preset blends**. That single choice addresses the diversity complaint directly.

## AI helps as a translator and prior, not as a per-user trainer

Feedback in language is the most practical AI-based, non-rating channel. **LLM2Fx** has an LLM map natural language to EQ and reverb parameters zero-shot, as JSON. For EQ it beat a CLAP-optimization baseline (MMD **0.22 vs 0.55**) ([LLM2Fx demo](https://seungheondoh.github.io/llm2fx-demo/)). **Text2FX** steers effects with CLAP plus differentiable DSP, and improved listener ratings in **67–75%** of cases ([Chu et al., ICASSP 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.18847)). **CTAG** fits the 78 parameters of a modular synth to a text prompt, and the parameters stay interpretable ([Cherep et al., ICML 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00294)). A conversational front end fits the existing procedural engine without replacing it. A listener says "less whine at high speed, more punch on throttle", and the LLM, given the parameter schema, proposes deltas that BO then refines. The evidence comes from effects processing, not EV synth timbre, and CLAP has no validated vocabulary for vehicle sound. Treat this channel as exploratory.

Population-scale alignment is the wrong tool for single sessions. **MusicRL** needed **about 300,000 pairwise preferences** from deployed users to train its reward model ([Cideron et al., ICML 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04229)). Tango 2 used DPO on a synthetic preference set ([Majumder et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09956)). Neither personalizes within a 30-minute session, and both assume a neural generator. Meta's open **Audiobox Aesthetics** predicts production quality and enjoyment for speech, music and sound ([Meta, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05139)). It could prune obviously poor candidates, but it encodes general quality, not personal taste, and it has not been validated on cabin sound. Pre-session questionnaires based on music-taste models such as MUSIC help music recommenders with cold start ([Soleymani et al., ICME 2015](https://webspace.science.uu.nl/~veltk101/publications/art/SMART2015.pdf)). No link to timbre preference has been shown. A "pick your favourite of four archetype clips" warm-up is a cheaper and more direct way to start the search. Implicit signals (replays, skips) and physiological signals have **no validation for personalizing synthesized sound** and do not justify the session time.

## Granular stays, but grains must follow the vehicle state, not a playhead

Granular synthesis is a sound foundation. Hyundai's e-ASD recombines very small sound units in real time from vehicle signals, and the company names "harmonically implementing multiple tones" as its advantage ([Hyundai Motor Group Tech](https://tech.hyundaimotorgroup.com/article/adding-sound-to-a-quiet-electric-vehicle/)). Siemens' case study describes Hyundai's grain position, pitch and duration varying with speed, motor speed, load and throttle ([Siemens/Hyundai](https://resources.sw.siemens.com/en-US/case-study-hyundai)). In games audio, Crankcase REV uses granular synthesis explicitly "to eliminate looping" ([Audiokinetic](https://www.audiokinetic.com/products/plug-ins/crankcase-rev/)), and a KTH listening test rated granular the most realistic engine-sound method ([af Malmborg](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1557027/FULLTEXT01.pdf)).

Audible looping usually means grain read positions repeat on a fixed period. The engine-sound literature instead **selects grains by state**. Jagla et al. store single engine cycles and recombine them with pitch-synchronous overlap-add to follow arbitrary speed profiles ([JASA 2012](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-00730614)). AudioMotors reproduces the engine cycle pitch-synchronously ([MCV UK](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/how-to-make-racing-car-engines-roar-using-audiomotors-fmod)). **CataRT** generalizes the idea. It analyzes a corpus into short units with descriptors (pitch, centroid, loudness), then picks the k nearest units to a moving target point in descriptor space, weighting each descriptor by its corpus standard deviation ([Schwarz et al., DAFx 2006](https://dafx.de/paper-archive/details/NJTiiDRZJmF9nDRvdE6qmg); [Einbond et al. 2009](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/16733/1/Einbond09.pdf)).

For EV Sound Lab this suggests a direct replacement for preset blending. Map speed, load, pedal and regen to a target descriptor vector. Pick nearby units with randomized read position and inter-onset interval. Overlap them with constant-power windows, and run independent layers with decorrelated seeds. Pitch-synchronous grains suit tonal layers such as motor whine. Asynchronous jittered grains suit texture layers such as wind. Adding a source file then enlarges a continuous corpus instead of adding another preset to blend. Doerfler and Wyse found that the same RPM sounds different under acceleration and deceleration, and conditioned on RPM deltas ([arXiv 2603.09391](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09391)). Feeding d(speed)/dt or the sign of pedal and regen into the grain mapping is the analogous step here.

The fixed global grain configuration is the clearest flaw. In **Lazaro et al.'s Hyundai-affiliated study, every varied parameter (source, grain duration, grain envelope, RPM range) shifted how refined, sporty and futuristic a 32-person jury rated the 27 stimuli** ([JAES 2022](https://aes2.org/publications/elibrary-page/?id=21566)). Liu et al. go further and optimize the grain window shape itself with a genetic algorithm ([SAE 2025-01-0253](https://saemobilus.sae.org/papers/sound-granular-synthesis-method-sci-fi-sound-quality-electric-vehicles-2025-01-0253)). A workable split follows from this, as project interpretation. Grain size range, window shape, pitch-sync on/off and usable read region are **per-source** settings, stored with the corpus. Layer mix, density, spray, the pitch-vs-speed curve, spectral tilt and the load-to-brightness mapping are **per-user** dimensions for the search. Headroom, smoothing time constants and maximum grain rate stay **fixed engineering limits**.

Generative AI is most useful offline, as a source of material. **Stable Audio Open** was trained only on Creative Commons data ([Evans et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.14358v2)), which makes it the most defensible choice for academic use. Prompt sweeps such as "low electric motor hum" or "metallic resonant drone", filtered for stationarity and then by ear, can fill the corpus with new raw material while the real-time engine stays native C++. Neural real-time synthesis is a later, optional tier. DDSP models conditioned on CAN-bus RPM, pedal and speed now exist for vehicles ([Choi et al., AES 2026](https://dabinkim0.github.io/publications/ddsp-carsound/index.html)). RAVE runs about 20× faster than real time on a laptop CPU, but needs streaming and causal export to avoid clicks ([Caillon & Esling](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.05011)). None of the 2024–2026 neural engine papers reports latency, and all of them trained on combustion engines, so these models are not ready to sit in the driving loop.

## OEMs ship presets from expert juries, and drivers split into camps

Granular synthesis is one of two mainstream industry approaches, not the universal one. Siemens' active-sound-design material lists **"Granular synthesis | Order synthesis"** as alternatives ([Siemens webinar](https://www.plm.automation.siemens.com/media/global/en/Webinar-Active-Sound-Design-AVAS_tcm27-81791.pdf)). Most OEMs disclose their inputs, not their method:

| OEM | Disclosed approach | Customer personalization |
|---|---|---|
| Hyundai (Ioniq 5 N) | Granular e-ASD; themes Ignition, Evolution, Supersonic ([press kit](https://www.hyundai.news/eu/models/performance/ioniq-5-n/press-kit/press-information.html)) | Theme choice, volume |
| BMW (HypersonX) | Algorithm generated "over nine million sound variations"; experts picked the best ([BMWBlog](https://www.bmwblog.com/2025/04/14/bmw-hypersonx-neue-klasse-sounds/)) | Drive mode; real-time control on a concept car only |
| Porsche (Taycan) | "Authentic" sound from the car's own sources, developed over years in studio, road and track ([Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/campus/sound-pioneer-tobias-hillers-18780.html)) | Option on/off |
| Mercedes-EQ | Composed themes; Roaring Pulse sold over the air ([MBUSA](https://media.mbusa.com/news/the-eqs-design-for-all-the-senses)) | Paid themes |
| Xiaomi SU7 (HEAD acoustics) | "Multi-layered, data-driven" engine on speed, torque and RPM; tested for long-term pleasantness ([HEAD](https://www.head-acoustics.com/blog/data/sound-that-moves-you-head-acoustics-sound-design-for-xiaomi/)) | Profile switching |

Two lessons follow. First, the prototype's inputs (speed, torque or pedal, regen, drive mode) match what every disclosing OEM names. Second, **no OEM adapts sound to an individual from feedback**. Shipping personalization means choosing a preset and a volume. BMW's process of generating millions of variants and having experts select from them is the nearest industrial analogue to interactive search, but the selection is done by experts, not drivers. An in-loop, per-listener search therefore goes beyond documented practice.

Evidence on who prefers what is consistent in shape but thin in detail. Mündler and Carbon's review collects the studies ([Frontiers 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/mechanical-engineering/articles/10.3389/fmech.2022.974464/xml)). Maunder and Munday (N=30) found **three groups**: the largest wanted ICE-like augmentation, one wanted non-ICE augmentation, and one preferred the genuine EV sound. Valeri and Pietila found no single sound preferred overall. In Küppers' free-configuration demonstrator, only **14% chose no added sound**. Sontacchi et al. found that many people preferred quiet cars but still wanted load-dependent loudness, and reported descriptor differences by gender. Lanslots' V8 preference came from a sample that was 62.5% ICE drivers, a hint that **prior vehicle experience shapes taste**. Lennström et al. identified a **sensitive subgroup** that found high-frequency tones increasingly unpleasant as level rose. The review concludes that a clear EV sound recommendation "cannot yet be concluded" because customers "lack reference points". Two implications follow. Preferences are likely to drift as listeners hear more options, so the search must tolerate non-stationary feedback. And drive context (comfort vs sport) probably needs its own preference model, as OEMs already assume by tying themes to drive modes. Age, culture, personality and driving style have **no tested predictive link** to EV sound preference. Sample sizes run from N=10 to 65.

## Discomfort is mostly computable before anyone listens

Three discomfort mechanisms are well understood and each maps to a cheap check. The first is **clashing partials**. Plomp and Levelt showed dissonance peaks when two partials sit about a quarter of a critical band apart, and Sethares made total dissonance computable as a sum over all pairs of partials ([Sethares](https://sethares.engr.wisc.edu/consemi.html)). Two layers that follow speed with different pitch slopes sweep through those peaks, which plausibly explains "occasional" bad combinations that appear only at some speeds. The project-level rule is to give every tonal layer the same pitch-vs-speed law times a fixed small-integer or engine-order ratio, so the interval never drifts. Sethares dissonance can then be evaluated from parameter-derived partial lists at sampled speeds, in microseconds, without rendering audio.

The second is **unmasked tones**. Separately audible tonal components severely reduce pleasantness in EVs ([Logos dissertation](https://www.logos-verlag.de/tocs/9783832555900toc.pdf)), and standard tonality metrics explain this only partly ([Lennström et al. 2019](https://ince.publisher.ingentaconnect.com/content/ince/incecp/2019/00000259/00000006/art00070?format=bib)). The third is **loudness and sharpness**, the strongest annoyance predictors. Zwicker's psychoacoustic annoyance combines loudness, sharpness, roughness and fluctuation into one scalar. Its sharpness term switches on above **1.75 acum** ([MetricGate](https://metricgate.com/docs/psychoacoustic-annoyance/)), a natural starting threshold for "piercing". The open-source **MoSQITo** package computes ISO 532-1 loudness, DIN 45692 sharpness, roughness, and ECMA-418-1 prominence ratio and tone-to-noise ratio ([MoSQITo docs](https://mosqito.readthedocs.io/en/latest/source/reference/mosqito.sq_metrics.html)). That is enough to screen rendered speed sweeps offline.

The metrics are too weak to serve as the objective. On artificial sounds, Aures' pleasantness model correlated with listener ratings at only **r ≈ 0.23**, and it rewards tonality, the very trait that annoys in EVs ([JAIST](https://dspace.jaist.ac.jp/dspace/bitstream/10119/18909/4/paper.pdf)). No public EV cabin target values exist. The design that follows has layers. Parameter rules and Sethares dissonance reject candidates before rendering. Metric caps on a rendered sweep reject candidates before playback. A small annoyance penalty breaks ties without overriding preference. A learned feasibility model handles everything the metrics miss. That last layer has direct precedent in **constrained BO**, which multiplies expected improvement by the probability of feasibility ([Gardner et al., ICML 2014](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v32/gardner14)). Preference-based variants also exist: C-GLISp learns unknown constraints from pairwise queries ([Zhu et al.](https://pdf.arxiv.org/pdf/2106.05639)), and CrashPBO adds binary "crash" feedback to preferential BO ([Menn et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01776v1)). The existing "Neither · new pair" button is close to this already. Splitting it into "dislike" and "uncomfortable" would give a clean signal for training the feasibility model. Every filtered candidate should be logged with its rejection reason, so that constraint rejections stay distinct from listener choices in the session record. A master true-peak limiter and a per-candidate loudness cap remain the outer guard. Hearing limits (NIOSH **85 dBA over 8 h**; WHO **80 dBA up to 40 h/week**) sit far above normal listening levels ([NIOSH](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/noise/prevent/understand.html)). In practice the risk is startle and peaks, not dose.

## Recommended direction and questions for the team

The recommended direction keeps the granular engine and builds three changes into it. All three are proposals; none is implemented. **First, rebuild the sound space.** Replace preset blending with CataRT-style state-driven grain selection over a descriptor-analyzed corpus. Move grain size, window and pitch-sync to per-source settings. Enlarge the corpus with filtered Stable Audio Open renders. Then expose about 6–12 continuous per-user dimensions, starting with a random embedding and moving to a perceptually regularized VAE trained on full-space samples once the pipeline works. **Second, change the loop.** Run Sequential Line Search (or BO-as-Assistant) over that space with a slider during a looping scripted drive. Seed it with a four-clip archetype pick, and accumulate a Meta-PO-style population prior across participants, keeping a no-prior control condition. Treat an LLM text box as an optional exploratory channel. **Third, gate every candidate.** Apply ratio-locked layers and dissonance checks in parameter space, MoSQITo caps on rendered sweeps, and a learned reject classifier as a feasibility term. Run a short calibration study to tune thresholds, because no published EV values exist.

| Open question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Is the main complaint diversity (space) or convergence (loop)? | Decides whether to rebuild the corpus or the search first; a pilot with the old loop on the new space separates them. |
| Slider or 4–6 clip gallery while driving? | Serial listening makes galleries expensive; sliders may distract from driving. |
| One model per drive mode, or mode as an input? | OEMs and subgroup evidence suggest context changes preference. |
| How many prior participants before a population prior helps, and does it bias later ones? | Unknown for audio; affects study validity. |
| Which covariates to log (ICE vs EV ownership, gender, noise sensitivity)? | These are the only covariates with some support; the rest are hypotheses. |
| Primary outcome: satisfaction, time to convergence, or stability on retest? | No study shows PBO raises satisfaction over IEC; the team must define success. |
| How strict should comfort filters be before they remove sounds people like? | Metrics are weakly valid; over-filtering recreates the low-diversity problem. |
| Can neural timbre transfer (RAVE/DDSP) meet latency alongside BeamNG on lab laptops? | No published benchmark; decides whether it stays off the roadmap. |

## Conclusion

The problems the team reports have one root cause: the system searches the wrong space with the wrong instrument. Preset blending caps diversity no matter how good the optimizer is, and 1–7 ratings over dozens of parameters exhaust listeners before the search learns anything about direction. Rebuilding the space (a state-driven corpus with per-source grains) and changing the instrument (slider-based preferential BO with comfort gates) are separable steps, and the team should run them as separate experiments so it can tell which one moved satisfaction.

The project also has a research opening that industry has not taken. OEMs choose among millions of generated variants through expert juries and ship a few presets. Studies keep finding that drivers fall into distinct preference camps, yet no one has shown per-listener adaptation of state-dependent EV sound. A well-instrumented study that logs rejection reasons, prior-vehicle experience and convergence would add new evidence about whether personalization beats a good preset menu, not only a better prototype.
